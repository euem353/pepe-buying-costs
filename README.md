## Which PEPE are you actually buying?

This matters more than the platform choice.

PEPE is a deflationary meme coin launched in April 2023 on Ethereum, inspired by the Pepe the Frog meme created by Matt Furie. It's an ERC-20 token with a total supply of 420.69 trillion, no transaction tax, and an anonymous founding team. According to the token's distribution breakdown, 93.1% of supply went into a Uniswap liquidity pool with the LP tokens burned, and 6.9% sits in a multi-signature wallet reserved for listings, liquidity and bridging.

The original has no utility beyond being tradeable and having a recognisable name, which is exactly the point of a meme coin — and worth saying out loud before you spend money on one.

Here's the practical trap: multiple tokens use the name and symbol PEPE. Wallet risk scanners flag this explicitly, warning that several contracts share the same ticker, that some versions have mutable metadata, and that several PEPE-named pools on smaller chains have high holder concentration and thin liquidity. If you're buying through a search box that just says "PEPE," you may not be buying the one you think.

On a large exchange the listing is a known quantity, which removes that specific risk. On a DEX or wallet swap, verify the contract address against an established reference before you confirm anything.

## The PEPE-specific risk, stated plainly

PEPE is a speculative asset with no real fundamentals or utility, and it's priced on sentiment rather than cash flow. Anyone buying it should be clear that a total loss is possible.

On the recurring "$1 target" question, the arithmetic settles it: with a circulating supply of 420.69 trillion tokens, a $1 price would require a market capitalisation of roughly $420.69 trillion — more than the entire global equity market combined. That's not a bearish opinion, it's multiplication. Traders modelling an aggressive bull cycle have generally pointed at fractions of a cent instead.

Set a position size you can afford to see halve, and treat the fee decisions above as the part you actually control.

## Questions people ask before their first PEPE purchase

**Do I need to buy USDT first?** Not necessarily. Card and bank transfer routes convert fiat to PEPE directly. Buying USDT first and then trading PEPE/USDT is usually cheaper, since the card fee is avoided.

**Is buying PEPE with a card faster than P2P?** Yes, and it costs more. Card purchases typically complete in minutes; P2P depends on how quickly the seller confirms your payment, though confirmed transactions often settle within minutes to under an hour.

**Can I buy PEPE without KYC?** On a centralized exchange, no — verification is required for fiat channels and P2P. Non-custodial wallets and DEX swaps don't ask for ID, but you're then responsible for the contract address, the network and the slippage.

**What can I do with PEPE after buying it?** Hold it in your spot wallet, trade it against other pairs, move it to a personal wallet, or use it in the platform's earn products. If your plan is to sell within a month, keeping it on the exchange saves you two sets of network fees.

**How much does a small purchase cost in total?** Take the fee percentage on the funding route and add the spot trading fee if you're buying through an order book. A $100 card purchase at 3% plus a 0.10% spot fill lands around $3.10 in costs before the price itself.
